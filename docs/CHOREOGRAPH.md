## 1. The product in one paragraph

Choreograph is a guest-first **AI front desk and scheduling product for service businesses**. Its public **Try demo** experience demonstrates that product through **Juniper Cleaning, a fictional home-cleaning company**. After a short Juniper introduction, a visitor chooses **Continue with HubSpot** or **Continue without HubSpot**, then describes a cleaning job and asks practical questions about coverage or preparation. The assistant consults Juniper's documents, answers with inspectable sources, clarifies the request and prepares a structured proposal. The visitor reviews a catalogue-based quote and requests a suitable appointment, then switches to the business perspective to review, assign and approve it. PostgreSQL reserves the appointment. If the visitor connected HubSpot, the reviewed synthetic record syncs to their selected account and they can open the matching native CRM record. Continuing without CRM makes no CRM calls. Rescheduling, cancellation and completion finish the story. Business-document retrieval helps someone decide what to book and arrive prepared; it belongs inside this journey.

**Promise:** describe the job; turn it into a reviewed, organized appointment.

**Owner priority, 22 October 2026 — visual conversation first:** the assistant conversation is the main product workspace. Combine flexible free text, ready-made questions and interactive service, document, quote and calendar cards in the conversation. Scheduling should visibly reason over an already occupied fictional week, offer a suitable alternative and let the visitor choose it. The visitor's local timezone controls their isolated demo schedule; the previous fixed New York proposal is superseded. This is a product-plan revision, not an implemented chat or availability engine.

**Owner-confirmed positioning, 22 October 2026:** Choreograph is the broadly positioned product; Juniper Cleaning is the concrete company used to demonstrate it. The landing page speaks to service businesses, and Try demo clearly introduces the fictional cleaning company before the visitor acts as its customer or coordinator. Cleaning-specific service names, policies, documents, staff and catalogue belong to that example, not to the definition of the Choreograph brand. The transferable story is enquiry → guidance/clarification → reviewed quote → appointment request → coordinator approval → reservation → CRM outcome.

This is product positioning, not a requirement for a universal scheduling engine. V1 implements one coherent company, catalogue and workflow. There is no industry selector, business onboarding, tenant administration, custom workflow builder or second industry demo in this scope. Other service businesses are possible future adaptations; this demo does not establish that their rules or integrations are already supported.

**Who the portfolio should convince:** clients needing Python APIs, AI-assisted intake, document-grounded assistants, booking systems, transactional data, third-party integrations, dashboards and maintainable existing-product work. A demo is evidence of implemented capabilities, not paid client experience, business results or years in a role. LlamaIndex/pgvector/RAG are implemented with local/injected evidence; real-provider support and quality acceptance remain outstanding.

## 8. State and action model

Separate related states instead of one overloaded status:

- **Enquiry:** draft → needs clarification → proposal ready → awaiting review → approved/declined/withdrawn.
- **Quote:** revision-bound and unaccepted/accepted/superseded; price-affecting edits require a fresh review.
- **Booking:** confirmed → rescheduled version / cancelled / completed. A preference or draft is never confirmed.
- **CRM connection:** not connected/skipped → connecting → connected; denied, disconnected, expired or capacity unavailable. An operator-test connection is a separate controlled configuration, never a public fallback.
- **CRM sync:** pending → running → synced only for an authorized connected destination; retry scheduled, needs reconciliation, needs attention or retired. Skipped CRM produces no sync attempt and cannot become Synced.
- **Knowledge document:** processing → ready/failed; a ready replacement supersedes the prior version for new retrieval. Removed/retired documents cannot be newly retrieved.
- **Workspace:** active → retired/expired → cleanup pending → cleanup completed, with failures reported separately.

Every mutating action has a workspace identity, current revision, narrow intent and idempotency key. Duplicate submissions return the established result. Stale actions explain that the record changed and offer a fresh review. The assistant and manual controls use the same backend commands.

Cancellation of an AI HTTP request does not prove a provider stopped billing. Retire its result from the UI immediately, while retaining conservative server admission accounting until actual completion/deadline. External mutations already in flight require reconciliation rather than an untrue “nothing happened” claim.

## 9. AI behavior and boundaries

OpenAI interprets and communicates; application code decides and persists.

Supported AI work:

- Extract a typed, bounded service request from free text.
- Ask relevant clarifying questions and offer contextual replies.
- Retrieve current business-document passages for service/policy questions, answer only what they support and show verified source references.
- Prepare revisions to a request, requested window, service or add-on.
- Explain an exact server quote or eligible-slot result.
- Prepare a supported reschedule/cancel action for explicit review.
- Summarize this guest's approved jobs and explain source-backed calendar/sync status.

Never let the model invent a service, price, discount, staff member, appointment or successful CRM update. It cannot run SQL, arbitrary HTTP calls, shell commands, external messages or workspace-wide deletion. Treat enquiries, uploaded documents, retrieved passages and any CRM text as untrusted content, not instructions that can expand tool permissions.

Tools should be a small task vocabulary such as search business knowledge, read catalogue, inspect request, propose request changes, calculate quote and find available slots. Search accepts a bounded question; the backend supplies authorized workspace and ready-version scope. Only application-confirmed actions call the actual mutation command. Do not make a model send a second differently shaped proposal after a deterministic tool already prepared the reviewable action.

Validate tool arguments and normalized responses with Pydantic/domain schemas. Resolve supported IDs from the current workspace. Preserve request/revision references and rejection codes. Show proposals separately from execution receipts. Ordinary conversation can be conversational; it need not create a modification to prove the assistant is useful.

One backend-owned provider transport handles deadlines, bounded response reading, allowlisted tool names and typed error categories. Use current official API schemas during implementation. A valid new optional provider field must not break the whole transport simply because a historical decoder rejected it. Application command contracts remain strict. Distinguish refusal, missing information, unsupported request, malformed response, timeout and outage.

Fake development output follows the same UI/domain pathway and is labelled appropriately. A live provider failure never silently becomes a fake success. Samples are ordinary inputs; only explicit processing dispatches a paid request.

### 9.1 Why RAG belongs in Choreograph

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) means finding relevant passages in company documents and supplying them as evidence for the assistant's reply. It is not training a custom model. The product need is that customers ask what a service includes, how to prepare and what a policy means before they can confidently request a booking. The coordinator should be able to update that guidance without editing prompts or application code.

The chosen implementation is **LlamaIndex's open-source Python framework + pgvector in the already planned PostgreSQL database**. LlamaIndex is limited to ingestion/retrieval and evidence assembly. The existing OpenAI assistant remains the conversational/action layer; avoid adding a second agent, duplicate answer-generation call or orchestration framework merely to demonstrate a library. pgvector stores/searches embeddings; it is not the source of booking truth. LangChain, a separate vector service and a hosted LlamaIndex subscription are not required.

A tiny hardcoded FAQ alone would not justify this machinery. The meaningful demo is the combination of question-driven retrieval, source inspection, versioned document replacement, honest missing-evidence handling and continuation into a real booking workflow. Keep the corpus realistic and small rather than padding it to imply scale.

### 9.3 Ingestion and retrieval, kept small

1. **Load and validate:** seed the ready sample pack through a deliberate preparation step. For guest replacements, initially support text-based PDF, UTF-8 TXT and Markdown; validate actual format and bound bytes/pages/extracted text. 2. **Preserve provenance:** retain the source/version/content hash, actual PDF page or text section and extracted text. Chunk within page/section boundaries, with a small measured overlap; keep the mapping needed for the viewer. A library's citation helper alone does not prove a correct page citation.
3. **Embed and store:** use a chosen supported embedding model through a server-only adapter and LlamaIndex's PostgreSQL integration. Record model, embedding dimension and chunker version. 4. **Retrieve when useful:** for company-specific guidance, query only authorized ready document versions and pass a small bounded set of passages to the answering assistant. Add a reranker or approximate index only when measured need justifies it.
5. **Answer and validate evidence:** include source IDs with the context, then validate that returned citation IDs belong to those passages. A similarity score is not a calibrated truth probability. Insufficient evidence, conflicting policies or unsupported requests trigger clarification/handoff. Verify factual support through evaluation, not citation syntax alone.
6. **Keep actions separate:** any quote, availability lookup or booking action uses the normal domain tools and review requirements. A request can combine one grounded policy answer with one exact tool result; their provenance remains distinct.
